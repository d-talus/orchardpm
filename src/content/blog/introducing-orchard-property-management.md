Orchard Property Management is proud to introduce itself to the owners, managers, and boards who keep New Jersey property running. We partner with established management companies across the state, and we hold them for good.

That last part does most of the work in this business. A management firm earns its reputation across years of returned calls, clean reconciliations, and meetings that finish on time. The firms we admire have spent a decade or more building something that works, and they tend to be wary of what a sale would do to it. They are right to be.

## Why we started

I grew up in New Jersey in a family built on hard work. My grandparents on both sides immigrated here and worked in the trades, which is probably why I find owner-run businesses more interesting than anything I encountered in private markets investing. Over the past several years I have invested in and worked alongside more than ten private, founder-owned companies, and the pattern repeats almost every time.

The owner has built something genuinely good. The staff have been there for years. The clients renew without being chased. And when the owner starts thinking about what comes next, the options on the table all look like a version of the same thing: a buyer with a fund timeline, a plan to consolidate, and a five-year horizon that ends in another sale.

We founded Orchard Property Management to put a different option on the table. We buy good firms, we keep the people, we keep the name, and we have no reason to sell again.

## What we look for

We partner with four kinds of company across New Jersey. Residential management firms handling single-family rentals, small apartment buildings, and mixed portfolios. Community association managers serving homeowners associations, condominiums, and co-ops. Commercial and mixed-use managers running office, retail, flex, and industrial property. And the maintenance and facilities operations that keep all of it in working order.

What those have in common matters more than what separates them. Each earns revenue that renews rather than revenue that has to be won again every quarter. Each depends on people who are genuinely hard to replace. And each rewards the firms that answer the phone and follow through, which is a harder standard to hold than it sounds.

As a rough guide, we look at firms with annual revenue of about $1 million or more and a profitable track record. We would rather hear from a strong smaller company than miss it, so treat that as a starting point rather than a gate.

## What we put behind a firm

Owners tell us the same two constraints over and over. The first is accounting capacity, because association books and owner statements do not wait, and one departure leaves a real hole. The second is manager capacity, since every new community or building means more evening meetings for the same people.

We fund both. Orchard adds accounting depth centrally, recruits and trains managers continuously rather than in a scramble, and covers credential and continuing education costs for the people working toward them. Behind that sits the executive support that comes with partnering with Talus Holdings: corporate development, finance and accounting, legal, lean implementation, human resources, tax, sales, and marketing.

Talus is neither a private equity firm nor a venture capital fund. With no investment timeline and no external shareholders, decisions follow what the business needs. That is the structural reason we can promise continuity and mean it.

## What stays the same

A firm that joins Orchard looks the same to a client the day after closing as it did the day before. Same name on the agreement. Same manager at the board meeting. Same phone number, same portal, same reporting package.

We prioritize retaining employees in every acquisition, and we keep managers with the communities and buildings they know. In this industry, a manager who remembers why a roof was phased over three years and how the board voted on it saves real money and real argument. That knowledge usually walks out the door in an acquisition. We treat keeping it as the first commitment we make.

## Who we would like to hear from

If you own a New Jersey management firm and you are thinking about what happens next, we would like to talk. The first conversation is a phone call, usually about thirty minutes, and it covers what you have built and what you want to happen. Nothing leaves Orchard, and we sign a mutual non-disclosure agreement before you hand over a single financial statement.

Plenty of the owners we speak with are years away from selling. Those conversations are often the most useful ones, because there is time to tidy the financials, document the management agreements, and develop the next layer of leadership before anything happens.

If you work in property management, we would also like to hear from you. Our companies hire maintenance technicians, licensed trades, community association managers, property managers, accountants, and leasing staff across New Jersey.
